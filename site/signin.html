The sign-in behind the gate

Approving or withholding a record is the one action on this site that changes what the public can see, so it sits behind a named account. This is a mockup of what that sign-in would look like.

Why Google and Apple

Because it means this project never handles a password. There is no password field on this page and there is no password column in the database — the identity provider authenticates the person and hands back a token. One less thing to lose.

Both are supported out of the box by Supabase Auth, which is already the platform in the proposal, so this is configuration rather than custom code.

The option that matters more

For Unit staff, the right answer is almost certainly the Unit’s own State of California account rather than a personal Google or Apple login — so that access follows the person’s employment, and leaving the Unit removes it automatically. That is a conversation with whoever runs identity for the department, and it is why that third button is shown greyed out rather than left off. Google and Apple cover the volunteer side and anyone outside the state directory.

Roles

RoleHeld byCan do
Unit reviewer Unit-designated staff Approve, withhold, redact, and take down. The only role that can make anything public.
Archivist The volunteer — me Ingest scans, catalog, describe, and flag for review. Cannot approve anything.
Unit viewer Anyone at the Unit Browse the full private catalog, including withheld items. No write access.
Public Not signed in Approved records only. Cannot see that a withheld record exists.

Note that the archivist — the person doing all the work — is deliberately the role that cannot publish. That separation is the whole point of the gate.

What sits behind it

  • Row Level Security decides what each role may read, in the database rather than the application. A signed-out visitor is a role with almost no permissions.
  • No shared logins. Every approve, withhold and redact is written to an audit trail against a named person with a timestamp.
  • Sessions expire. Access is removed by the Unit at any time, without needing anything from me.
  • Masters and unredacted originals live in a private bucket reachable only by short-lived signed URL. Approving a record does not move the master.
